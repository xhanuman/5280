## What Exactly is a Sprinkler Startup?

A sprinkler startup, also known as spring activation or de-winterization, is the formal process of bringing your lawn's irrigation system back online after the cold winter months. It is the direct counterpart to winterization, where water is blown out of the lines to prevent freezing and cracking. The startup procedure must be done methodically to avoid a destructive phenomenon called "water hammer," where a sudden surge of water pressure can crack pipes, fittings, and sprinkler heads.

Beyond simply preventing damage, a professional startup is a critical annual check-up for your entire system. According to the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), a single household's irrigation system leaks can waste up to 6,300 gallons of water per month. A thorough startup inspection is the first line of defense against such waste, identifying and fixing leaks before they impact your water bill and the environment.

## When is the Best Time for a Sprinkler Startup in Denver?

Timing your sprinkler startup in Denver is a delicate balance. Activating the system too early risks damage from a late-season freeze, while waiting too long can stress your lawn as temperatures begin to rise. For the Denver metropolitan area, the optimal window is typically between **late April and mid-May**.

This timing is based on historical weather data. The Old Farmer's Almanac places Denver's average last spring frost date around May 10th. By waiting until at least the last week of April, you significantly reduce the risk of water in your backflow preventer or pipes freezing and causing catastrophic damage. It's always wise to check the 10-day forecast before scheduling your activation to ensure no surprise cold snaps are on the horizon.

## Can I Perform My Own Sprinkler Startup?

For homeowners with a good understanding of their irrigation system, a DIY sprinkler startup is achievable. However, it requires careful attention to detail to avoid damaging the system. If you choose to do it yourself, follow these steps precisely:

1.  **Check the Backflow Preventer:** Before introducing any water, ensure the two small test cock valves on your backflow preventer are closed. They are typically screwdriver-slotted.
2.  **Locate the Main Shutoff Valve:** Find the main water supply valve for your sprinkler system, which is usually located in your basement or a crawl space.
3.  **Open the Main Valve SLOWLY:** This is the most critical step. Turn the handle only a quarter of the way to allow water to enter the mainline very slowly. You should hear water hissing as it fills the pipes. Wait several minutes for the sound to stop, indicating the pipes are full.
4.  **Fully Open the Main Valve:** Once the pipes are full, you can slowly open the valve the rest of the way.
5.  **Check for Leaks:** Go outside to your backflow preventer. Inspect it closely for any drips or spraying water. If you see leaks, shut the water off immediately and call a professional.
6.  **Open Backflow Valves:** Slowly open the ball valves on the backflow preventer, one at a time.
7.  **Test Each Zone:** Go to your sprinkler controller and manually run each zone for 2-3 minutes. As each zone runs, walk your property.
8.  **Inspect Sprinkler Heads:** Look for leaks around the base of each head, damaged or broken heads, clogged nozzles creating erratic sprays, and heads that don't pop up correctly.
9.  **Adjust and Program:** Adjust any misaligned sprinkler heads to ensure they are watering your lawn and not the sidewalk. Finally, program your controller with a new watering schedule appropriate for spring conditions, adhering to any local Denver Water restrictions.

## Common Problems Found During a Sprinkler Activation

After a long, cold winter, it's common to uncover issues during the spring startup. An experienced technician can quickly diagnose and resolve these problems:

*   **Broken or Damaged Sprinkler Heads:** Often caused by snowplows, mowers, or winter freeze-thaw cycles.
*   **Leaking Valves:** A valve that doesn't fully close will cause a specific zone to run constantly or lead to soggy spots in the lawn.
*   **Underground Pipe Leaks:** Freeze damage can cause cracks in the PVC pipes, leading to pressure loss and large wet areas.
*   **Clogged Nozzles:** Dirt and debris can clog sprinkler nozzles, resulting in poor spray patterns and dry spots.
*   **Controller Malfunctions:** Old controllers can fail, batteries can die, and wiring can become disconnected over the winter.
*   **Low Water Pressure:** This can indicate a hidden leak, a partially closed valve, or an issue with the backflow preventer.
