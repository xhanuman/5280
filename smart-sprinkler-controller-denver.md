## What Is a Smart Sprinkler Controller?

A smart sprinkler controller — also called a WiFi sprinkler controller — is an advanced irrigation timer that connects to the internet. Unlike traditional timers that run a fixed schedule regardless of conditions, a smart controller pulls real-time local weather data (temperature, rainfall, humidity, wind) and automatically adjusts watering accordingly. This process uses a principle called evapotranspiration (ET) to ensure your landscape gets exactly what it needs — no more, no less.

Beyond rain delays, smart controllers factor in:

- **Plant type** — Grass, shrubs, trees, and flower beds all have different water needs.
- **Soil type** — Denver's clay-heavy soils retain water longer than sandy soils, which matters for run time and cycle scheduling.
- **Sun exposure** — Full-sun zones need more water than shaded areas.
- **Nozzle type** — Precipitation rate of each head affects how long each zone should run.
- **Slope** — Sloped areas require shorter, more frequent cycles to prevent runoff.

The EPA's WaterSense program certifies controllers that meet efficiency standards. A 2021 EPA study found that WaterSense-labeled controllers save nearly 7,600 gallons annually for the average home.

## How to Upgrade to a WiFi Sprinkler Controller

### Step 1: Assess your current system
Count your irrigation zones — this determines which model to buy. Controllers come in 4, 6, 8, 12, and 16-zone versions. Also confirm you have a solid WiFi signal (at least two bars) at your current controller's location.

### Step 2: Choose the right controller
Match zone count first, then decide: Rachio for simplicity and smart home integration (by far the overwhelming favorite), Hunter for professional-grade features and complex systems (less than 1% of my clients use this).

### Step 3: DIY or professional installation?

**DIY:** Turn off power to the old controller, photograph the existing wiring, label each wire, remove the old unit, mount the new one, reconnect wires to their terminals, and configure zones via the app. Doable if you're comfortable with basic wiring.

**Professional:** The hardware swap is the easy part. What matters is zone configuration — soil type, plant type, nozzle precipitation rate, and slope for each zone. Incorrect settings mean the controller runs on bad assumptions. A professional installer gets this right from the start, which is where the real water savings come from.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much water can I actually save?**
Most Denver homeowners see a 30–50% reduction in outdoor water use. For an average property, that's several thousand gallons and $100–$300 per season, depending on lot size and current habits.

**Are there smart controller rebates from Denver Water**
Denver Water has historically offered rebates for WaterSense-certified controllers. These programs change year to year — check the official Denver Water website for current availability, qualifying products, and how to apply.

**What happens if my WiFi goes down?**
The controller runs the last saved schedule from onboard memory. It won't make real-time weather adjustments until connection is restored, but watering continues.

**Do I still need to winterize with a smart controller?**
Yes. The controller manages scheduling — it doesn't affect your pipes and heads. Blow-out service each fall is still required to prevent freeze damage during Denver winters.

**What does a WiFi sprinkler controller upgrade cost?**
The controller itself runs $150–$300 depending on brand and zone count. Professional installation in the Denver area typically adds $200–$500, covering hardware swap, wiring, and full zone configuration.
