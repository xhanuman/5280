## Centennial's Unique Sprinkler Challenges: Soil, Pressure, and Climate

Maintaining an efficient sprinkler system in Centennial requires understanding the specific local conditions that stress your system.

### The Challenge of Alkaline Clay Soil

Centennial's soil is predominantly **clay-based and tends toward alkaline** — typical of Front Range soils — creating two major irrigation challenges:

1.  **Poor Drainage:** Clay soil compacts and drains slowly. Over-watering causes runoff and root damage, requiring precise zone timing and low-precipitation heads.
2.  **Mineral Buildup:** High alkalinity causes faster buildup of scale in heads and nozzles, leading to clogs, uneven spray patterns, and reduced efficiency.

### Managing Variable Water Pressure

Centennial's water pressure is highly variable depending on neighborhood and elevation.

*   **High Pressure:** Pressure in some neighborhoods can exceed 75 PSI. This causes water to mist, leading to significant waste and putting undue stress on components, resulting in premature failure and leaks.
*   **Low Pressure:** Peak demand can cause low pressure, resulting in poor spray distance and uneven watering.

Our technicians are experts at diagnosing pressure issues and installing or adjusting **Pressure Regulating Valves (PRVs)** to ensure your system operates at the optimal PSI, protecting equipment and conserving water.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Q1: Why is my sprinkler system leaking or constantly running?
**A:** Common causes are a broken main line, a damaged head, or a faulty valve. High water pressure often stresses older components, leading to leaks. We perform a full system audit to isolate the exact cause.

### Q2: How often should I water my lawn in Centennial's climate?
**A:** Due to clay soil, water **less frequently but for longer durations** to encourage deep root growth. We recommend 20-30 minutes every two to three days, in the early morning (4 AM to 8 AM) to minimize evaporation and reduce fungal risk.

### Q3: What is a Pressure Regulating Valve (PRV) and do I need one?
**A:** A PRV reduces and maintains constant water pressure. Highly recommended in Centennial due to variable pressure, it protects your system from surges, prevents misting (which wastes water), and extends the life of your heads and pipes.

### Q4: Why are some of my sprinkler heads not popping up?
**A:** Usually caused by low water pressure, a clogged nozzle, or a broken riser. Low pressure can be due to a leak or a zone valve problem. Clogs are common due to mineral deposits. We clean or replace nozzles and check water flow to restore proper function.

### Q5: When is the best time to schedule my sprinkler winterization (blowout)?
**A:** Winterization is critical to prevent pipes from freezing. The ideal time is **between late September and the end of October**, before the first hard freeze. We use a high-volume, low-pressure air compressor to safely clear all water from the lines, which is essential to protect your investment.

### Q6: Can you help me save money on my water bill?
**A:** Absolutely. We achieve water conservation savings by fixing leaks, installing Smart Controllers (which adjust watering based on real-time weather), optimizing nozzles for uniform delivery, and programming your system for "deep and infrequent" watering.
