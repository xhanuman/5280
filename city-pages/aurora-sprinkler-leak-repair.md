## City-Specific Sprinkler Challenges in Aurora

Aurora's location on the high plains of Colorado subjects irrigation systems to a combination of environmental factors that demand specialized knowledge. Ignoring these local nuances can lead to costly repairs, water waste, and a struggling landscape.

### 1. The Challenge of Swelling Clay Soil

A significant portion of the soil across the Aurora area, particularly in newer developments, is composed of **bentonite clay**, often referred to as "swelling soil" [1]. This highly expansive soil absorbs large amounts of water, causing it to swell and put immense pressure on underground pipes and sprinkler lines.

| Soil Condition | Impact on Sprinkler System | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Expansive Clay** | Shifts and stresses buried pipes, leading to cracks and leaks, especially at joints. | Professional leak detection and use of flexible, high-grade piping materials for repairs. |
| **Poor Drainage** | Water pools, leading to over-saturation, root rot, and inefficient watering cycles. | Adjusting sprinkler heads for proper coverage and implementing cycle-and-soak programming. |

When this soil dries out, it shrinks, which can cause further movement and damage. This constant cycle of expansion and contraction is a primary cause of underground leaks and broken lines in Aurora's irrigation systems.

### 2. Managing High Water Pressure

Many Aurora neighborhoods receive water from the Aurora Water utility, and while pressure is generally well-regulated, some areas experience **higher-than-average static water pressure** [2]. While high pressure might seem beneficial, it can be detrimental to a sprinkler system:

*   **Misting and Fogging:** High pressure causes water to atomize, leading to significant water loss through evaporation and poor distribution.
*   **Premature Wear:** It accelerates the wear and tear on sprinkler heads, seals, and internal components, leading to frequent breakdowns.
*   **Pipe Stress:** Excessive pressure can strain older or weaker pipes, increasing the risk of a burst line.

Our technicians are experts in diagnosing and installing **pressure-regulating devices** and heads to ensure your system operates at the optimal PSI (pounds per square inch), maximizing efficiency and system longevity.

### 3. The Critical Need for Winterization

Aurora's climate is characterized by cold, dry winters with sudden, deep freezes. The need for proper **sprinkler blowouts and winterization** is non-negotiable [3]. Any water left in the pipes, valves, or backflow preventer will freeze, expand, and inevitably crack the components, leading to catastrophic damage when the system is turned on in the spring. We provide timely, professional winterization services to protect your investment from the harsh Colorado winter.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) for Aurora Homeowners

### Q1: When should I have my sprinkler system blown out in Aurora?

**A:** The ideal time for a sprinkler blowout in Aurora is typically between **mid-September and the end of October**. The goal is to complete the winterization process before the first hard, sustained freeze, which can occur unexpectedly in the Denver Metro area. Waiting too long risks water freezing in the backflow preventer or lines, leading to expensive repairs in the spring. We recommend scheduling your blowout early in the fall to secure your spot.

### Q2: Why is my water bill so high, and how can 5280 Sprinklers help?

**A:** A sudden spike in your water bill is often the first sign of a hidden leak. In Aurora, leaks are common due to the expansive clay soil shifting underground. We can perform a **comprehensive water audit and leak detection service**. This involves checking for visible leaks, using specialized tools to find underground leaks, and evaluating your controller's programming to ensure you are not overwatering. We often find that simple adjustments to run times and the installation of pressure-regulating heads can significantly reduce consumption.

### Q3: What is a backflow preventer, and why does it need to be protected during winter?

**A:** A backflow preventer is a critical device required by Aurora Water that prevents contaminated water from your irrigation system from flowing back into the public drinking water supply. It is typically located above ground and contains water, making it extremely vulnerable to freezing. If the water inside freezes, the internal components and brass body can crack. As part of our winterization service, we drain, insulate, or remove the backflow preventer to ensure it is protected from freeze damage.

### Q4: How often should I run my sprinklers in Aurora's climate?

**A:** Due to Aurora's semi-arid climate and the need for water conservation, it is more effective to water **less frequently but for longer durations** (deep and infrequent watering). This encourages deeper root growth, making your lawn more drought-tolerant. We recommend following the Aurora Water guidelines, which often suggest watering 2-3 times per week, depending on the season and weather. Our smart controller programming service can automate this process based on real-time local weather data.

### Q5: I have low pressure in one zone but not the others. What could be the cause?

**A:** Low pressure in a single zone is a classic symptom of a few common issues. The most likely causes are a **partially closed or failing zone valve**, a **significant leak** in that specific zone's line, or a **clogged filter or nozzle**. Our technicians will systematically check the valve for debris or diaphragm failure, inspect the line for breaks, and clean or replace any clogged components to restore full pressure and coverage.

### Q6: Can I fix a broken sprinkler head myself?

**A:** While replacing a simple pop-up head might seem straightforward, it's easy to make mistakes that lead to long-term problems. Improper installation can result in a head that is too high or too low, leading to poor coverage and potential damage from lawnmowers. More importantly, a DIY repair may not address the underlying cause, such as high water pressure or a main line issue. We recommend professional service to ensure the correct head type is used, the pressure is regulated, and the repair is durable.

### Q7: What is the benefit of a "cycle and soak" watering schedule?

**A:** Aurora's clay-heavy soil has a low infiltration rate, meaning it can't absorb water quickly. If you water for a long period all at once, most of the water will run off into the street, wasting water and causing erosion. A **cycle and soak** schedule breaks the total watering time into shorter intervals (e.g., three 5-minute cycles instead of one 15-minute cycle), with a 30-60 minute "soak" period in between. This allows the water to slowly penetrate the soil, ensuring deep root watering without runoff. We program all smart controllers with this efficient method.
